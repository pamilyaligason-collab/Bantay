If something goes wrong with your data

Draft, not yet approved. The family admin reviews this page before the word Draft is removed. Last updated 2026-10-27.

A data breach means someone who should not see the family's records could, or did. A lost phone with the app signed in, a leaked password or a mistake in the service can all do it. This is what the family admin does.

1. Stop it (same day)

  • Sign the affected phone out, or remove the person from the family list so their PIN stops working.
  • Change the passwords and keys of the service involved (Supabase, Google Drive, OpenRouter, Cloudflare).
  • Write down what happened, when it was found, and which records could be involved.

2. Find out how big it is (within 24 hours)

  • Which people and which kinds of records (readings, papers, messages, location).
  • Whether anyone outside the family could read them, and for how long.

3. Tell the people affected

Tell each affected family member in plain words: what happened, which of their records, what the admin did, and what they should do (for example, change their PIN). Do it within 72 hours of finding out, and sooner when their health or safety could be at risk.

4. Tell the National Privacy Commission when required

The Philippine Data Privacy Act asks for a report to the National Privacy Commission, usually within 72 hours, when sensitive personal information such as health records may have been acquired by an unauthorised person and there is a real risk of serious harm. The family admin decides this with the facts from step 2 and keeps the reasoning in writing, even when the answer is no.

5. Fix it and learn from it

  • Close the gap that let it happen, and test that it is closed.
  • Add a line to the family's decision log: what happened and what changed.
