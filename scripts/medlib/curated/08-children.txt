mp: Fever
aka: fever, lagnat, nilalagnat, mainit ang bata, febrile
wiki: Fever
s: Fever is the body fighting an infection. It is a sign, not an illness by itself.
s: How sick the child looks matters more than the number.
s: Fluids and rest help. Watch for the warning signs.
g: A baby under 3 months with a fever of 38 C or more
g: Stiff neck, fits, a rash that does not fade when pressed, or very hard to wake
g: Fast or hard breathing, blue lips, or cannot drink
g: Fever for 2 days or more, or a child who looks very ill
what: A fever is a body temperature of 38 C or more. It often comes with colds, flu, ear infections, dengue and other infections. Most fevers in children settle in a few days. Look at how the child acts. A child who plays and drinks is usually less worrying than one who is limp and will not drink.
signs: Hot skin, flushed face, chills ; Tired or cranky ; Not wanting to eat ; Sweating when the fever breaks
home: Give plenty of fluids, breast milk or oral rehydration salts ; Light clothes and a cool room, no cold baths ; Paracetamol by the weight on the pack if the child is uncomfortable ; Never give aspirin to a child ; Check often and see a doctor if unsure ; Do not rub with alcohol
prev: Wash hands often ; Keep vaccines up to date ; Keep sick children home ; Clean up standing water against dengue

mp: Dehydration
aka: dehydration, nauubusan ng tubig, tuyo, nanunuyo ang labi, ORS, hilo sa init
wiki: Dehydration
s: Dehydration means the body has lost more water than it has taken in.
s: It is common with diarrhoea, vomiting, fever and hot weather.
s: Babies, small children and older people get dry fastest.
g: Very sleepy, confused or hard to wake
g: Cannot drink, or keeps vomiting everything
g: No urine for 8 hours (baby: no wet diaper for 6 hours), sunken eyes, cold hands
g: Fast heartbeat with fainting
what: When we lose water through sweat, vomit or loose stools and do not drink enough, the body runs dry. Mild dehydration can be treated at home with fluids. Severe dehydration is an emergency, especially in babies and older people.
signs: Thirst and dry mouth ; Dark yellow urine, or very little ; Dizziness ; Tired and cranky ; Baby: sunken soft spot, no tears when crying
home: Sip small amounts often ; Use oral rehydration salts (ORS) made up as the pack says ; Keep feeding breast milk to babies ; Rest in a cool place ; Wait ten minutes after a vomit, then start sips again
prev: Drink water through the day, more in hot weather ; Start ORS at the first loose stool ; Do not wait to feel thirsty if you are old or working in the heat

wiki: Breastfeeding
s: Breast milk alone is the best food for a baby for the first 6 months.
s: Start in the first hour after birth. Feed often, day and night.
s: Most mothers can make enough milk with support.
g: A baby with no wet diaper for 6 hours, or very sleepy and will not feed

aka: febrile seizure, kombulsyon, nanginginig sa lagnat, kombulsyon ng bata
wiki: Febrile_seizure
s: Some young children have a fit when the fever rises quickly.
s: It looks scary but is usually harmless and lasts under 5 minutes.
s: Stay calm. Keep the child safe, and always get a check.
g: A fit that lasts more than 5 minutes: call 911 or go to hospital now
